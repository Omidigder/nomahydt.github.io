About

Keeping streamflow forecasts reliable in the face of growing uncertainty

I am a hydrological modeller working on the snowmelt-dominated basins of the Canadian Prairies. My work asks where forecast error actually comes from, then tests whether assimilating observations can reduce it.

My dissertation follows precipitation and parameter uncertainty through conceptual and machine-learning streamflow models, then benchmarks assimilation algorithms in synthetic-truth experiments so that each source of error can be isolated.

The research is funded by Manitoba Transportation and Infrastructure. During a year at the Hydrologic Forecast Centre I built Raven models for the Assiniboine and Red River basins and automated the model-building workflow used for operational flood forecasting.

Research

Four threads, one question

Uncertainty

Precipitation and epistemic error

Separating the share of streamflow uncertainty caused by precipitation estimates from the share caused by model structure and parameters, using multi-model and multi-parameter ensembles.

Assimilation

Ensemble Kalman Filter and generative DA

Updating Raven model states with the EnKF and Generative Ensemble Data Assimilation, benchmarked in perfect-DA experiments with a known synthetic truth.

Cold regions

Snowmelt-driven streamflow

Simulating Prairie basins with several conceptual structures and diagnosing how each one represents snow, soil and runoff processes differently.

Data and ML

Satellite products and LSTM models

Evaluating gridded precipitation against gauges, and comparing conceptual and LSTM models for how strongly each reacts to precipitation estimate and forecast errors.
