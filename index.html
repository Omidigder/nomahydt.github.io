A hydrologic digital twin for data-sparse northern Manitoba

NoMaHyDT combines multi-source Earth observations, reanalysis data and data assimilation to keep a virtual copy of the Churchill River basin in step with the real one, and to advance streamflow prediction where ground data are scarce.

Project description

Floods cause larger property losses in Canada than any other natural hazard. In northern Manitoba, the Churchill River basin supports hydroelectric generation and northern and First Nations communities, and lies beside the Hudson Bay Railway and the Port of Churchill, the only deepwater Arctic port linked to the North American rail network.

Hydrological models can support flood outlooks here, but the region is data-sparse: few weather stations and stream gauges cover very large areas, and snow and soil moisture are poorly monitored. Satellite and reanalysis products give full coverage, but their errors vary by season and region, and no single product is best everywhere.

What NoMaHyDT adds

NoMaHyDT builds an automated, near-real-time pipeline that merges satellite and reanalysis data into one model-ready dataset with uncertainty estimates. It drives the Raven hydrological framework, used at the Manitoba Hydrologic Forecast Centre, and assimilates satellite snow and soil moisture every day so the model's states stay consistent with the real basin.

Forecasts and what-if scenarios issued from these updated states are shared on an open web platform, labelled as a research prototype and reviewed with the Hydrologic Forecast Centre and Manitoba Hydro before release.

How the digital twin works

A digital twin is a virtual replica of a physical system that is continuously updated with observations and linked to decisions through a two-way feedback loop. NoMaHyDT runs this loop every day:

Observe

Gauges, stations, satellites and reanalysis describe the real basin.

Merge

Products are combined into one dataset with uncertainty and ensembles.

Assimilate

An ensemble Kalman filter updates Raven's snow and soil moisture states.

Forecast

Streamflow forecasts and what-if scenarios start from the updated states.

Decide

Users act; the effects appear in the next day's observations.

Loop concept after Ghaneei and Moradkhani (2027) and Pal et al. (2025).

Objectives

Objective 1

Near-real-time data pipeline

  • Stream gauges (Water Survey of Canada) and weather stations (ECCC, Manitoba Hydro)
  • Precipitation: IMERG, GSMaP, MSWEP, ERA5, MERRA-2, JRA-3Q, CaPA
  • Soil moisture (SMOS, SMAP), snow (MODIS), water storage (GRACE/GRACE-FO), surface water (SWOT)
  • Automated download, quality control, regridding and formatting
Output: automated, documented pipeline and historical archive
Objective 2

Uncertainty and merging

  • Uncertainty of each product by season and region
  • Statistical and deep-learning merging weighted by uncertainty
  • Forcing ensembles for data assimilation and forecasting
Output: gridded, model-ready forcing dataset with uncertainty estimates
Objective 3

Raven modelling and data assimilation

  • Raven models built with the HFC automated workflow
  • Calibration on streamflow, snow and soil moisture; GRACE checks the water balance
  • Ensemble Kalman filter for satellite snow and soil moisture
  • Hindcasts comparing data sources, with and without assimilation
Output: calibrated Raven models with DA and a hindcast test of forecast skill
Objective 4

Digital twin platform

  • Daily pipeline, assimilation and forecasts
  • Map interface to view and download data, states and forecasts with uncertainty
  • What-if scenarios defined with users
  • Labelled a research prototype, not an official forecast
Output: the platform, documentation and user feedback

Study area

The Churchill River basin covers roughly 296,000 km² of boreal ecoclimatic region and is sparsely gauged. It is the second major river system of the Nelson–Churchill River Basin, which drains about 1.4 million km² to Hudson Bay.

How the river's discharge, regulation and estuary ice interact near the Port of Churchill is not well understood. Better streamflow estimates for the basin and its unregulated tributaries could help address this.

Why it matters: the 2017 flood

Spring floods in 2017 reportedly washed out the Hudson Bay Railway track bed at 19 locations, and it took 18 months to reconnect Churchill. Air freight cost up to five times more than rail, War Lake First Nation declared a state of emergency, and the port ceased almost all shipping.

Permafrost thaw and seasonal flooding continue to put pressure on the corridor.

Expected impact

Flood forecasting

Better inputs and continuously updated states could support provincial flood early warning.

Hydropower

Tested forecasts could support operations by Manitoba Hydro in these basins.

Port and railway

Flow forecasts can help plan maintenance and schedule shipments around high-flow periods.

River mouth and estuary

Flow estimates could inform environmental assessments, including beluga habitat and coastal ice.

Northern communities

Open data and forecasts support water-related decisions in remote First Nations communities.

Other basins

The open workflow can be reused in other data-sparse basins in Manitoba and beyond.
